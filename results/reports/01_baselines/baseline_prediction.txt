3. EPISODE-LEVEL vs WINDOW-LEVEL -- WHY THEY DISAGREE
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Window-level Test_F1 (0.0939 +/- 0.0636) and Test_PR_AUC (0.0934 +/- 0.0580)
look poor in isolation, but they are measuring the wrong thing for a RAS
cueing use case. Pre-FoG positive windows are only ~1-4% of all windows in
every fold (n_pos/total ranges from 0.54% (S06) to 4.06% (S05) -- see
results/reports/prediction_smoke.txt section 2 for the full per-candidate
breakdown), versus DETECTION's ~19% positive rate (Daphnet's native 81/19
split, S6). This 5-15x sparsification is an INTRINSIC consequence of the
task definition (horizon=window_size=64, step_size=32 -> each onset
contributes at most ~2 positive-labeled windows by construction), not a
preprocessing defect -- confirmed correct in ADR-014 and the smoke test.
Sample-wise F1/PR-AUC are known to collapse under this level of imbalance
even when the underlying detector is doing something useful minute-to-minute
(CLAUDE.md S6: "Sample-wise F1 alone is not clinically meaningful").

For a closed-loop RAS cueing system, what matters is NOT "does every single
64-sample window score correctly" but "does the system fire at least once,
with enough lead time to cue, before the freeze happens." That is exactly
what episode_recall and mean_lead_time_s measure. By that standard, the
PREDICTION model is meaningfully informative: 69.8% of onsets (unweighted
mean across folds; 73.4% pooled) get caught with an average 0.72s of lead
time out of the 1.00s horizon -- i.e., when it fires, it tends to fire early
in the horizon, not right at the edge of onset. Test_ROC_AUC (rank-based,
not threshold-dependent) stays much healthier than F1/PR-AUC for the same
reason (mean 0.7404, comparable in absolute terms to several DETECTION
folds) -- ROC-AUC does not collapse the same way under class imbalance that
F1/PR-AUC do.

Treat window-level F1/PR-AUC in this report as SPARSITY-LIMITED, secondary
diagnostics -- not the headline for judging whether PREDICTION is viable.

with the two tasks picking up genuinely different parts of the movement
signal (pre-onset preparation vs. during-freeze tremor/akinesia).
