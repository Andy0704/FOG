All three headline metrics improved (F1 +0.071, PR-AUC +0.049, ROC-AUC +0.049)
AND all three std shrank (F1 std 0.206 -> 0.144, PR-AUC std 0.151 -> 0.108,
ROC-AUC std 0.172 -> 0.126). The fixed-val protocol validated on S01 in 6 of 8
folds (val_subj=pool[0] always defaulted to the alphabetically first non-test
subject); the inner-LOSO protocol now distributes val_subj across S01
(3 folds), S06 (4 folds), and S03 (1 fold) -- see findings below.
Higher mean AND lower variance together indicate the old fixed-val selection
was not just biased but was picking a systematically worse (or at least
noisier) early-stopping/threshold point than a genuinely representative val
subject would.

inner_scores findings
----------------------------------------------------------------------
(a) S02 had the HIGHEST inner ROC-AUC score in every single fold where it was
    a candidate (all 7 outer folds except test=S02 itself: 0.9515, 0.9623,
    0.9555, 0.9617, 0.9614, 0.9638, 0.9628 for test=S01/S03/S05/S06/S07/S08/S09
    respectively) -- yet S02 was NEVER selected as val_subj (0 of 8 folds).
    This is exactly the failure mode the median rule is designed to avoid:
    S02 is consistently the "luckiest" val candidate (easiest to validate on),
    and a MAX-rule would have picked it every time, silently inflating the
    reported baseline with an easy validation subject instead of a
    representative one.

(b) S01 no longer dominates the val role. Under the old fixed-val protocol,
    val_subj = pool[0] defaulted to S01 in 6 of 8 folds (any fold where the
    test subject was not S01 itself). Under inner-LOSO, S01 is selected in
    only 3 of 8 folds (test=S02, S03, S06); S06 is now the most frequent
    selection (4 of 8: test=S01, S07, S08, S09); S03 is selected once
    (test=S05). No single subject captures anywhere near 75% of the val role
    anymore.

(c) S08 is the LOWEST-scoring inner val candidate in every fold where it was
    a candidate (all 7 outer folds except test=S08 itself: 0.3491, 0.4183,
    0.3759, 0.4941, 0.4338, 0.3990, 0.4973 for test=S01/S02/S03/S05/S06/S07/S09
    respectively) -- consistently the worst subject to validate on, in
    addition to being the worst subject to TEST on (Test_ROC_AUC=0.4908,
    flagged below). This is consistent with the akinetic-freezing phenotype
    finding in docs/case_study_S08.md: whatever makes S08 hard to generalize
    TO also makes it hard to validate/early-stop ON, i.e. this is a genuine,
    consistent property of S08's data, not an artifact of the outer-fold
    test-time evaluation alone.
