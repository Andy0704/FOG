>>> TRIAL 33 (THE BEST TRIAL) IS NaN-AFFECTED. <<<
Trial 33 had 5 of its 21 candidate trainings diverge to NaN loss (fold=S01
candidates S07 @epoch16 and S08 @epoch18; fold=S08 candidates S03 @epoch16,
S06 @epoch17, S07 @epoch14). This is flagged LOUDLY as instructed.

HOWEVER -- important nuance verified below (not just asserted): whether
this actually CORRUPTS the reported 0.6260 value depends on whether the
SELECTED (median-rule) candidate for each fold was among the diverged
ones, and whether the returned probabilities for that candidate were
computed from a NaN-corrupted model or a rescued pre-divergence checkpoint.

**Verification of the rescue mechanism:** trainer_tcn.py only saves a
checkpoint when `val_loss < best_val_loss` -- any comparison with NaN is
False in Python, so once a candidate's val loss goes NaN, NO further
checkpoint save ever occurs and the LAST GOOD (pre-divergence) checkpoint
is what run_split() reloads and evaluates. This mechanism only fails if a
candidate's very FIRST epoch is already NaN (no checkpoint would ever
exist to reload). Checked: the EARLIEST any candidate in the entire study
went NaN was epoch 8 (multiple instances) -- every affected candidate had
at least 7 valid epochs first, so a rescue checkpoint always existed.
Cross-checked against results/optuna/trials_log_full_prauc.csv: ZERO
missing/NaN values anywhere in the CSV (118 fold-rows total), and every
fold_median_val_prauc value is a normal, plausible in-range number (min
0.3015, max 0.6659, no exact-0 or out-of-[0,1] degenerate values that would
indicate a corrupted probability array reached the scorer). This is strong
evidence the checkpoint-rescue held throughout the study -- no trial's
reported PR-AUC number is a raw NaN or a garbage value.

For TRIAL 33 SPECIFICALLY: its S08-fold selected_val_subj was "S03"
(score=0.6443) -- and S03 WAS one of the candidates that later diverged
(NaN at epoch 16). So trial 33's reported value is NOT independent of the
NaN event: it is the rescued pre-epoch-16 checkpoint's score for that
candidate, not a fully clean 25-epoch convergent run. The S01-fold
(selected=S05, score=0.6659, S05 never went NaN) and S03-fold (selected=S09,
score=0.5677, S09 never went NaN) are unaffected. **Verdict: trial 33's
0.6260 is not a corrupted/garbage number, but 1 of its 3 fold scores comes
from a training run that later became unstable and had to be rescued by
early-stopping's checkpoint mechanism, rather than a run that trained
cleanly start to finish.** Treat it as usable but not fully trustworthy for
"this exact hyperparameter combination trains stably" -- see section 4.

Total: 16/40 trials NaN-affected (any candidate); of those, 10/40 trials
had the SPECIFIC selected/scoring candidate itself go NaN (i.e., the
reported number for that fold was checkpoint-rescued, not from a clean
run) -- trial 33 is one of these 10.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

Std of all 37 COMPLETE trial values:    0.0408

The top 5-10 trials form a fairly tight CLUSTER (spread of ~0.011 among
the top 5), sitting meaningfully above the field median (~0.027 gap, more
than half a standard deviation) -- so the tuning IS finding a real,
non-trivial region of better-performing hyperparameters, not pure noise:
architecture (K3/[1,4,8] and K3/[1,2,8] over the baseline K5/[1,2,4]) and a
mid-to-upper lr band both show up consistently among the leaders, not just
in one lucky trial.

BUT within that top cluster, the ranking between trial 33 (0.6260) and the
best CLEAN trial 26 (0.6173, a gap of only 0.0087) is NOT clearly
distinguishable from noise once NaN-rescue risk is accounted for --
0.0087 is smaller than the top-5 spread itself (0.0113) and much smaller
than the field's std (0.0408). Practically: pick the best CLEAN trial (26)
or a similarly-clean, similarly-scoring trial (e.g. 37, also clean,
0.6132, SAME architecture as trial 33) as the safer choice for a stable
production run, rather than trial 33's nominally-highest-but-rescued
number. The apparent "win" of trial 33 over a clean trial like 26 is
small enough that it plausibly reflects which run's checkpoint got
luckily rescued right before its best epoch, not a genuinely superior
hyperparameter combination.

5. LEARNING-RATE FLAG (for any future study's search-space design)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
lr range searched: [1e-4, 3e-3] (log scale). Split at the geometric-ish
practical threshold of 1e-3 (roughly upper-third of the log-scaled range):

  NaN-affected trials (n=16): lr range [0.001051, 0.002880], ALL 16/16
    have lr > 1e-3.
  Clean trials (n=21):        lr range [0.000117, 0.002041], only 5/21
    have lr > 1e-3.

This is a near-PERFECT separation: every single NaN-affected trial used
lr > 1e-3; only a minority of clean trials strayed into that range (and
those that did, e.g. clean trial with lr=0.00204, evidently got lucky with
its specific arch/dropout/batch_size combination). FLAG FOR ANY FUTURE
STUDY: strongly consider lowering the upper lr bound from 3e-3 to
somewhere around 1.5e-3-2e-3, or adding gradient clipping to trainer_tcn.py
(a training-code change, out of scope for this read-only task) if the
search needs to keep exploring lr > 1e-3 territory. Right now roughly 40%
of trials are "spending" compute on runs that partially diverge, which is
wasted search budget even though the checkpoint-rescue mechanism prevents
outright corruption of the reported numbers.
