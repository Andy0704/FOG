B. DECOUPLED INFERENCE CADENCE (ADR-017) -- infer_step SWEEP
----------------------------------------------------------------------
edge/stream_infer.py gained --infer_step (default 8), independent of the
training stride STEP_SIZE=32 (still used only by the training pipeline in
src/). The 64-sample causal window and model weights are unchanged; only
how often a forward pass fires changes. Training data/labels untouched --
this only affects the DEPLOYED replay script.

Replay: data/dataset/S01R01.txt (92802 raw samples, 18 onsets), same
checkpoint/threshold as part A, causal post-proc (median k=5 +
majority_vote w=7, reused unchanged from src/run_loso.py) for all three
settings.

infer_step | decision_interval_ms | n_fwd_passes | cue_triggers | sanity_recall | mean_lead_time_s
-----------|----------------------|--------------|--------------|----------------|------------------
32         | 500.00               | 2899         | 58           | 0.7222 (13/18) | 0.6899
16         | 250.00               | 5797         | 82           | 0.8333 (15/18) | 0.8760
8          | 125.00               | 11593        | 226          | 1.0000 (18/18) | 0.8490

Effective causal post-proc span in ms (HONEST accounting -- k=5/w=7 count
DECISION steps, not raw samples, so their real-time reach SHRINKS as
infer_step shrinks):

infer_step | decision_interval_ms | median(k=5)_span_ms | majority_vote(w=7)_span_ms | combined_worst_case_ms
-----------|----------------------|----------------------|------------------------------|-------------------------
32         | 500.00               | 2500.00              | 3500.00                      | 5500.00
16         | 250.00               | 1250.00              | 1750.00                      | 2750.00
8          | 125.00               | 625.00               | 875.00                       | 1375.00

DOES SMALLER infer_step IMPROVE LEAD TIME / RECALL ON THIS REPLAY? YES,
clearly, on both:
  - sanity_recall:     0.7222 -> 0.8333 -> 1.0000  (monotonic improvement)
  - mean_lead_time_s:  0.6899 -> 0.8760 -> 0.8490  (large improvement from
                        32->16; a small, non-monotonic dip from 16->8, still
                        far above the infer_step=32 baseline)

BUT this comes with a real, honestly-reported cost: cue_triggers rises
sharply (58 -> 82 -> 226) as infer_step shrinks. Part of this is genuinely
"catching more onsets, correctly" (recall going to 18/18), but part of it
is the causal post-proc's smoothing/debounce window shrinking in real time
(combined span 5500ms -> 1375ms at k=5/w=7 fixed) -- the same k/w values
smooth over much less real-world history at infer_step=8, so the binary cue
signal is structurally chattier (more separate on/off blips) even where the
underlying onset count (18) hasn't changed. This is NOT a bug -- it is the
direct, expected consequence of decoupling infer_step from the (k=5, w=7)
post-proc that was originally tuned around STEP_SIZE=32's smoothing span --
flagged explicitly for follow-up in section D below rather than silently
picking infer_step=8 as a free win.

C. LATENCY -- FORWARD (MEASURED) vs DECISION INTERVAL (STRUCTURAL),
   REPORTED SEPARATELY, NOT MERGED
----------------------------------------------------------------------
*** WSL/RTX4060 BASELINE NUMBERS. NOT JETSON FIGURES. Same harness reruns
unchanged on Jetson tomorrow. ***

edge/latency_harness.py now sweeps the same infer_step values and reports
forward compute time (measured) and decision_interval (structural) as two
SEPARATE numbers -- never summed into one "end_to_end" figure that would
hide which part is actual compute vs which part is a config choice.

device=cuda (RTX 4060), n_steps ~2879-11573 (20 warmup iters dropped/setting):

infer_step | forward_mean_ms | forward_median_ms | forward_p95_ms | decision_interval_ms (structural)
-----------|------------------|---------------------|-----------------|-------------------------------------
32         | 1.6615           | 1.4006              | 3.3503          | 500.0000
16         | 1.6261           | 1.4019              | 3.3265          | 250.0000
8          | 1.6655           | 1.4185              | 3.3758          | 125.0000

device=cpu, same replay:

infer_step | forward_mean_ms | forward_median_ms | forward_p95_ms | decision_interval_ms (structural)
-----------|------------------|---------------------|-----------------|-------------------------------------
32         | 1.2728           | 1.2037              | 2.2601          | 500.0000
16         | 1.2100           | 1.1843              | 2.0263          | 250.0000
8          | 1.2410           | 1.2057              | 2.1054          | 125.0000

Forward compute time is EMPIRICALLY FLAT across infer_step (as expected --
a single forward pass costs the same regardless of how often it's called;
the ~0.02-0.04ms variation across settings is measurement noise, not a
real effect). What actually changes with infer_step is the structural
decision_interval, which the table keeps as a visibly separate column
rather than folding it into the compute number. CPU is again marginally
faster than GPU here (same finding as edge_scaffold.txt part 1) -- expected
for a model this small at batch_size=1, where GPU kernel-launch overhead
dominates actual compute.

- ONNX export UNBLOCKED and PASSED: max abs diff = 1.192e-07 (<< 1e-4).
  onnx==1.22.0 and onnxruntime==1.27.0 installed and added to
  requirements.txt.
- infer_step is now decoupled from the training stride STEP_SIZE=32 (which
  remains untouched in src/ and in the training pipeline). Shrinking
  infer_step 32->16->8 clearly improves the streaming replay's sanity
  recall (0.7222->0.8333->1.0000) and mean lead time (0.69s->0.88s->0.85s),
  at the cost of ~4x more forward passes (still cheap, ~1.2-1.7ms each,
  same order as before) and a much chattier cue signal (58->82->226
  trigger events) because the SAME k=5/w=7 post-proc window now spans much
  less real time (5500ms->1375ms combined). RECOMMENDATION FOR TOMORROW: do
  not just adopt infer_step=8 as a free win -- either re-tune k/w for the
  new decision cadence (e.g. scale k/w up so the real-time smoothing span
  stays roughly constant across infer_step) or add an explicit refractory/
  debounce period in edge/ras_cue.py before committing to a final
  infer_step for the live demo. This is a genuine, not-yet-resolved
  tradeoff -- flagged, not silently decided.
- Latency harness now reports forward (measured, ~1.2-1.7ms, essentially
  infer_step-independent) and decision_interval (structural,
  infer_step-dependent: 500/250/125ms) as two SEPARATE numbers, never
  merged -- the earlier edge_scaffold.txt's "end_to_end" framing is
  superseded by this clearer separation (see DECISIONS.md ADR-017).
- No changes to src/ or data/; all work stayed within edge/ (plus the two
  new lines in requirements.txt).
