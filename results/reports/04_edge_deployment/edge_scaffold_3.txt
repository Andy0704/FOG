1. WHAT CHANGED
----------------------------------------------------------------------
edge/stream_infer.py: added derive_kw(infer_step, median_span_ms=800,
majority_span_ms=1000) -- k/w are now DERIVED per infer_step so
causal_median(k)/causal_majority_vote(w)'s REAL-TIME span stays close to
the target spans instead of shrinking as infer_step shrinks (the cause of
edge_scaffold_2.txt's cue-trigger explosion, 58->226). Explicit --k/--w
still override the derivation if needed (e.g. to reproduce the old fixed
k=5/w=7 behavior).

edge/ras_cue.py: RASCueEngine gained refractory_ms (default 2000ms) --
after a cue event fires, any rising edge within refractory_ms is suppressed
(counted, not emitted as a new event). edge/stream_infer.py now reuses
RASCueEngine UNCHANGED to compute the WITH-refractory trigger count
alongside the raw rising-edge count.

Both causal_median/causal_majority_vote themselves are still the EXACT
SAME functions from src/run_loso.py, reused unchanged -- only k/w (which
were always caller-supplied parameters) are now computed differently.

3. THE KEY QUESTION: DOES SMALLER infer_step STILL HELP ONCE SPAN IS
   HELD CONSTANT?
----------------------------------------------------------------------
TRIGGER-COUNT EXPLOSION: YES, IT GOES AWAY. Raw trigger count now ranges
122-134 across the full infer_step sweep (a 1.10x spread) versus
edge_scaffold_2.txt's fixed-k/w sweep, which ranged 58-226 (a 3.90x
spread). This directly confirms span-constant post-proc controls cue
chatter as intended.

RECALL: NO, IT DOES NOT KEEP IMPROVING -- it is already at CEILING (18/18)
at infer_step=32 once span is held constant, stays at ceiling at
infer_step=16, and actually dips slightly to 17/18 at infer_step=8 (one
onset flips from hit to miss; with only 18 total onsets this single flip
is within plausible sampling noise, not a clear trend). This is a
materially different picture from edge_scaffold_2.txt's fixed-k/w sweep,
where recall rose monotonically 0.7222->0.8333->1.0000 as infer_step
shrank. Comparing the two sweeps side by side reveals WHY: fixed-k=5/w=7
at infer_step=32 produced a 5500ms combined smoothing span -- so long it
was likely OVER-SMOOTHING and diluting real onset signal; shrinking
infer_step under that fixed-k/w regime incidentally shrank the span back
toward a more reasonable range, and MOST of the earlier "smaller infer_step
helps recall" trend was probably this de-dilution effect, not a genuine
cadence benefit. Once span is explicitly held ~constant (not shrinking as
a side effect), recall is already saturated at the largest infer_step
tested.

LEAD TIME: YES, a real (if modest) cadence benefit remains -- mean lead
time improves 0.7240s -> 0.8351s -> 0.8667s as infer_step shrinks, even
with span held constant. This makes intuitive sense: finer decision cadence
lets the system register a threshold-crossing closer to the moment it
actually happens, rather than waiting up to decision_interval_ms extra
before the next opportunity to decide -- a genuine, not-confounded cadence
effect, distinct from the (now resolved) smoothing-span story.

BOTTOM LINE ON THE KEY QUESTION: the trigger-count explosion is fixed.
Recall gains from smaller infer_step were substantially a smoothing-span
artifact, not a real cadence effect -- once controlled for, recall doesn't
keep improving. Lead time IS a genuine, if modest, cadence benefit.

4. REFRACTORY / DEBOUNCE EFFECT (refractory_ms=2000, default)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
infer_step | cue_triggers_RAW | cue_triggers_WITH_refractory | n_suppressed | pct_suppressed
-----------|--------------------|---------------------------------|-----------------|------------------
32         | 122                | 117                              | 5               | 4.1%
16         | 125                | 112                              | 13              | 10.4%
8          | 134                | 123                              | 11              | 8.2%

The refractory period trims RAW trigger counts by only ~4-10% -- a real
but MODEST cleanup, not a fix for the underlying volume of triggers. HONEST
FLAG: with only 18 true onsets in this replay but 112-123 triggers even
after refractory, the large majority of triggers are NOT clustered tightly
around the 18 known onsets (a 2000ms refractory would suppress rapid-fire
bursts effectively if that's what was happening) -- they appear to be
scattered elsewhere through the ~1450s replay, consistent with the
window-level PR-AUC already being known to be low for this task
(results/reports/baseline_prediction.txt: mean Test_PR_AUC=0.093+/-0.058).
This is a PRECISION problem in the underlying probability stream, not
something span-constant post-proc or refractory debounce can fully solve
on their own -- flagged for follow-up (e.g. re-examine the frozen
threshold=0.176353, or PROJECT_STATUS.md sec4 item 11's sparsity-driven
tuning ideas), not silently smoothed over by this report.

5. RECOMMENDATION FOR TOMORROW'S JETSON DEMO (FOR APPROVAL, NOT AUTO-COMMITTED)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Recommended: infer_step=16, k=3, w=4 (derived, span-constant),
refractory_ms=2000.

Reasoning:
  - infer_step=16 matches infer_step=32's ceiling recall (18/18) while
    giving a meaningfully better mean lead time (0.8351s vs 0.7240s --
    roughly 11% of the 1.00s horizon closer to the true onset), a real
    cadence benefit per section 3.
  - infer_step=8 does NOT clearly outperform infer_step=16: its recall is
    actually slightly worse (17/18 vs 18/18, likely noise but not a
    reason to prefer it) and its lead time gain over infer_step=16 is
    small (0.8667s vs 0.8351s, +0.0316s) for roughly 2x the forward-pass
    compute (11593 vs 5797 passes) and the highest raw trigger count
    (134) of the three settings.
  - infer_step=16's decision_interval (250ms) is still comfortably fast
    for a cueing system, and forward compute cost is negligible either
    way (~1.2-1.7ms/pass on WSL/RTX4060, see edge_scaffold_2.txt sec C;
    Jetson TODO: confirm this holds on-device).
  - refractory_ms=2000 (the specified default) is kept: it gives a modest,
    real reduction in trigger count (10.4% at infer_step=16) with no
    observed downside (it operates strictly on TIME between events, never
    changes which onsets get caught -- recall/lead-time numbers above are
    computed on the pre-refractory cue_binary stream, so refractory cannot
    have hidden a recall cost).

This does NOT resolve the section 4 precision flag (most triggers are not
tied to the 18 known onsets) -- that is a separate, still-open issue for
the model/threshold, not something infer_step/k/w/refractory_ms tuning can
fix. Recommend surfacing that explicitly before/alongside the Jetson demo,
not resolving it silently.
